Thursday with Mav: why I’m taking the power back

The first Thursday episode of Power Back Weekly. Mav on why The Q Collective exists, how the Q Score works, and what to watch before the Colorado legislature comes back on January 11. Here's something most people never hear. Every vote your state lawmaker casts is already public. Every single one. The problem was never that the record was hidden. The problem is that nobody was reading it out loud. So that's what we're going to do.

This is Power Back Weekly. I'm maveriQ B Jackson, founder of The Q Collective. Sundays, you get the record: what moved, what changed, whose score went up or down. Thursdays, you get me. In my own voice, no script reader, telling you what it means and what you can do about it. This is the first Thursday episode. So let me tell you why this exists.

I spent six years inside the federal government as a project coordinator. The Department of Justice. The IRS. The White House. The U.S. Senate. And here's what I learned: the people who know how the system works have a huge advantage over the people the system is supposed to serve. Not because they're smarter. Because they're paying attention, every day, and most of us can't. This year I ran for the Colorado House, in House District 23. I didn't win. And I'll be honest with you: what I took from it wasn't disappointment. It was clarity. You don't need a seat to hold power accountable. You need the record, and you need people who are willing to look at it. So The Q Collective became exactly that. A nonpartisan, citizen-powered group that reads the record for you, scores it the same way for everybody, and puts it where you can see it.

Let's talk about the Q Score, because you're going to hear about it a lot. Every Colorado lawmaker gets one number, from zero to one hundred. It's built the same way for every single one of them, no matter their party, no matter their district. It comes from what they actually did on the public record. Not what they said on a mailer. And we don't guess. A new lawmaker needs at least five recorded votes before they get a score at all. Every time a score changes, it's logged in a public audit log on our site, so you can check our work. If we ever get something wrong, we fix it, and we show you that we fixed it. We don't endorse candidates. We don't tell you how to vote. We don't take sides. We pick balance. If a bill only wins by making someone else lose, it isn't ready.

The Colorado legislature comes back on January 13. Between now and then, here's what's happening. We've written two bills of our own. CESA, the Colorado Everyday Stability Act, is built around seven pillars that make everyday life more stable for working families. And FWMRA, which says nobody gets fired for no reason, with costs employers can actually predict. Both are ready for sponsors, and both are explained in plain English on our site. The record was always public. Now you can actually read it. This is Power Back Weekly. I'm Mav. Take the power back.
